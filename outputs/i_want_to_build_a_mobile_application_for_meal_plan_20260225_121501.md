## Executive Summary

There is a clear and growing opportunity for a diabetes-focused meal planning mobile app: the diet & lifestyle/meal-planning subset of diabetes management apps is estimated at roughly $580M globally (2025) with North America representing about $200M and ~8.5% CAGR. The market already includes capable incumbents (SNAQ, mySugr, PlateJoy, Fooducate and others) that address parts of the problem—image-based carb counting, broad diabetes tracking or premium personalized meal plans—but none combine real-time device-driven forecasts, household/caregiver workflows, culturally-aware recipes and budget-aware shopping as a single, clinician-friendly product. Major risks are high: regulatory classification as a medical device, strong competitive moves from incumbents and device-makers, technical challenges in CGM integration and model accuracy, and privacy/compliance obligations. Recommended strategy is to pursue a focused, clinically-aligned niche (e.g., CGM users, caregiver/household mode or newly diagnosed Type 2s), validate with 1–3 clinical pilots, architect privacy-first serverless APIs that leverage third-party nutrition databases and LLMs for coaching, and differentiate through CGM-informed meal impact scoring, cultural/budget customization and clinician exports. With conservative execution and early pilot evidence, the product can capture a defensible SOM within 3–5 years while limiting regulatory exposure by clearly scoping claims or pursuing a formal regulatory pathway if needed.

## Risk Analysis

| Risk | Category | Severity | Mitigation |
|------|----------|----------|------------|
| Large incumbents and specialized diabetes platforms (e.g., Abbott, SNAQ) are launching free, AI-based meal-glucose prediction and meal-planning features that directly compete with a new diabetes meal-planning app. | competitive | 🔴 HIGH | Differentiate by focusing on a narrow niche (e.g., type 1 pediatric patients, cultural meal plans, or clinician-integrated workflows), pursue partnerships with clinics/dietitians and smaller CGM/SMBG vendors, and build exclusive data or features (e.g., local food databases, care-team integrations) that incumbents don’t provide. |
| Providing personalized glucose predictions or meal advice risks classification as a regulated medical device (and medical advice), triggering FDA/CE (EU MDR)/local medical device rules and associated clinical evidence requirements. | regulatory | 🔴 HIGH | Engage a regulatory consultant early to determine device classification, either design the product to stay within wellness/non-medical claims or plan a regulatory pathway with premarket submissions (FDA 510(k) or EU conformity) and build a clinical validation plan to generate required evidence. |
| Accurate meal-glucose prediction requires high-quality CGM/SMBG integration and validated ML models; poor predictions could harm users and damage trust. | technical | 🔴 HIGH | Prioritise integration with validated CGM/SMBG APIs/SDKs, run controlled pilot studies with real users to validate prediction models, implement conservative risk controls (clear confidence intervals, safety prompts, and clinician escalation paths), and version/monitor models continuously. |
| Handling users' health data (glucose, meals, meds) creates significant privacy/security and compliance obligations (e.g., HIPAA, GDPR) and increases liability if breached. | regulatory | 🔴 HIGH | Architect for privacy from day one: minimize stored PHI, encrypt data at rest/in transit, implement access controls and audit logging, sign BAAs where required, and adopt GDPR/HIPAA-aligned policies and breach response processes. |
| Users expect deep personalization and self-service; lack of highly-personalised, user-friendly meal plans and in-app self-management will reduce engagement and retention. | adoption | 🟡 MEDIUM | Invest in strong onboarding and personalization (diet preferences, cultural foods, carbs counting), use progressive profiling and ML to refine recommendations, and implement self-service tools and in-app education; run A/B tests and collect UX metrics to iterate quickly. |

### How Your Product Should Be Unique

- CGM- and device-driven meal planning: integrate real-time CGM, insulin-pump and wearable data to predict per-meal postprandial glucose excursions and auto-generate alternative meals or portion adjustments with a 'meal impact score' and 2-hour glucose forecast (no autonomous insulin dosing).
- Household & caregiver mode: multi-person meal plans that scale servings and macros separately for the person with diabetes and other household members, produce a single adaptive shopping list, and provide role-based sharing and one-tap clinician reports.
- Culturally-aware, budget-optimized recipes: cuisine filters and culturally authentic recipe banks plus a budget mode that substitutes lower-cost equivalent ingredients so weekly plans meet a user’s max grocery spend and glycemic-impact target.
- Clinically-validated glycemic metadata + barcode verification: include per-serving glycemic index/load and verified carb counts (barcode scanner), label recipes with predicted glycemic load, and make datasets exportable to clinicians while offering a low-cost core tier and affordable clinician-connected premium.

> **Positioning Statement:** For adults with diabetes who need practical, affordable meal plans that actually reduce post-meal glucose spikes and fit family life and budgets, GlyciPlan is a mobile meal-planning app that generates CGM-informed, RD-curated, culturally-aware and budget-optimized meal plans with household scaling and clinician export; unlike Peas & Hoppy, our product integrates real-time device data and local price/cultural customization to predict and minimize postprandial glucose for each meal.
